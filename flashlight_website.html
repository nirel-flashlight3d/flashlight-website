The challenge

Health information remains fragmented

Medical histories are spread across provider portals, diagnostic reports, wearable apps, genomic files, laboratory results, and personal records. That fragmentation makes it hard to see a complete, longitudinal view of your health.

Flashlight 3D is building tools for individuals to own, understand, and act on their own health data. One longitudinal record, assembled around the person it belongs to.

Medical Records

One place for all your health information.

Your records, lab results, wearables, and genetic data are scattered across dozens of apps and portals. Flashlight brings them together into one place that you own, then helps you actually understand them.

See the first tool → How your data is protected

How it works

What happens when you ask a question

In plain terms: you ask a question, Flashlight confirms it has your permission, gathers the relevant parts of your record, lets AI read only what it needs, and hands back a clear answer with its sources. You review it, and you decide what happens next.

1
Top row: your question, and the answer. Follow the light left to right as your question is handled, then back to you for review.
2
Bottom row: your data, in the background. Records arrive on their own and quietly build the record the answer draws on.
A
You ask
You ask a question in plain language, from any app you already use.
Working in the background

Connections gather records from your providers, labs, wearables, and genetic tests automatically. One inbox keeps everything together, and Filed safely reads, tidies, and stores each item as an untouched original, stamped with its date and source.

Always watching

Monitoring checks the whole system around the clock and flags anything missing, like a dropped record or an empty scan, so a person can step in.

● Current status

Medical Records is currently in development. The steps above describe what it is intended to do, and may change during research, testing, and validation.

How it works

From an unreadable VCF to a report you can actually use

Step 1

Upload

Provide a VCF file generated by a sequencing or genetic-testing service.

Step 2

Annotate

Using the same annotation service that powers the Flashlight platform, each variant is compared against trusted public genomic references, ClinVar, gnomAD, and a pharmacogenomics panel.

Step 3

Organize

Annotations are grouped and translated into more accessible language.

Step 4

Review

The report describes available annotations, possible relevance, evidence context, and important limitations.

Chippy · Internal AI agent

Built for the builders, before it's built for anyone else.

Six people and four months in, we were already losing track of our own decisions. So we built Chippy: an agent that answers questions from our own working documents, with sources attached.

How we handle information

In development · private testing

Chippy is being tested privately inside Flashlight 3D. It is not available outside the company.

Same architecture, different corpus

Chippy retrieves from the team's internal documents instead of a person's medical records or genome, but the underlying approach is the same.

Sourced, not recalled

Answers are grounded in stored documents and point back to where they came from, rather than being generated from memory alone.

● Why it exists

Flashlight is a small team building Medical Records and the Genomic Sequencing Pipeline at the same time. Chippy is our own proof that the shared retrieval architecture behind both products works, applied first to the problem closest at hand: keeping a fast-moving team from losing track of what it already figured out.

How it works

Input, curation, output

Input

Internal documents

Specs, meeting notes, research summaries, and decisions the team has already written down.

Curation

Retrieval over a curated corpus

Chippy indexes and retrieves from that material, rather than treating a language model's memory as the source of truth.

Output

Grounded, cited answers

Responses point back to the document they came from, so the team can check them the same way a user would check a health finding.

● Current status

Chippy is in development and currently being tested privately within Flashlight 3D. It is internal tooling, not a public product, and is not the company's main business.

Trust & Privacy

Clear consent, limited use, strong safeguards, and meaningful control.

Genomic and health information deserve careful handling. These are the commitments and principles that guide how Flashlight 3D builds.

Privacy

Genomic-data commitments

What we will and won't do with genomic information.

  • We do not sell genomic information.
  • We do not use genomic information for advertising.
  • We do not provide genomic information to employers, insurers, or data brokers.
  • We do not train public or shared AI models on uploaded VCF files without express consent.
  • Users can request deletion of uploaded files, reports, and account information.
  • Genomic information is processed only for disclosed purposes.

Responsible AI

Principles for AI and genomic information

How we keep AI outputs honest, bounded, and reviewable.

01

Informational, not diagnostic

Outputs describe available information; they do not diagnose or prescribe.

02

Clear source attribution

Every claim links back to the reference information it came from.

03

Visible uncertainty

Evidence strength and limitations are shown, not hidden.

04

Human oversight

Findings are meant to be reviewed with a qualified professional.

05

Privacy by design

Data minimization, disclosed purposes, and user control are built into the system from the start.

Jonathan Price

Founder

Jonathan founded Flashlight 3D to put a complete, understandable view of a person's health back in their own hands. His work sits at the intersection of health data, artificial intelligence, and individual agency.

How Flashlight started

In October 2025, I attended the MedTech conference in San Diego and was blown away by the vision on display: a connected hospital system where patient data flows seamlessly from wearables to doctors to the operating room, one intelligent system coordinating care for an entire community.

The following week, my dad called with bad news. His prostate cancer had returned and metastasized into his lymph nodes. The same week, his cardiologist told him his thoracic aneurysm had reached the threshold for open-heart surgery.

I asked him to share every hospital login he had. I downloaded as many of his medical records as I could, processed them in ChatGPT, and spent seven hours on my flight to Florida asking questions: understanding his history, his treatment options, the side effects of each, how they might interact, and how to prioritize.

I prepared reports. I went with him to his appointments. And I was struck by the gap between the MedTech vision and the reality on the ground: paper forms and clipboards asking the same questions every visit. Doctors without a complete picture. A one-hour drive to pick up a CD from one hospital and carry it to another. Incorrect information in his chart that affected his surgery criteria.

I set out to solve the problem for my dad, and to build something that scales to everyone who doesn't have a family member able to drop everything for a month to do this work.

So I started building. I started Flashlight 3D to pull together my passions and experience: data science, mentoring young developers, education and workforce development, health and life science, and saving lives.
